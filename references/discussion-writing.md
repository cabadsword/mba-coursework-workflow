# Discussion Writing

Use this reference for Canvas discussion posts, peer replies, and reflection-based MBA writing.

## Intake

- Confirm the prompt, minimum word count, citation requirements, number of peer replies, due dates, and whether first-person reflection is expected.
- Read prior drafts or posted text if available.
- Ask for personal background only when the prompt requires personal experience, self-assessment, career goals, or examples that cannot be inferred from course materials.

## Original Posts

Use a simple structure unless the prompt requires another format:

1. Direct answer or thesis.
2. Course concept explanation.
3. Personal or business application.
4. Scholarly/course source support.
5. Closing takeaway.
6. References, if required.

Keep the tone professional, MBA-level, and natural. Avoid overclaiming and avoid citations that were not validated.

## Peer Replies

Choose an easy-to-respond post when asked to propose a reply:

- Prefer posts with a clear thesis, concrete example, or one point that can be extended.
- Avoid posts that are too vague, hostile, or require unsupported correction.
- Write replies that add value: connect to a concept, ask a thoughtful question, or extend the example.
- Keep replies respectful and concise unless the rubric asks for depth.

## Non-Submission Rule

Draft in chat or files first. Do not post to Canvas unless the user explicitly instructs you to submit/post/reply.

## Final Checks

- Meets word count.
- Addresses every prompt question.
- Uses only validated sources.
- APA 7 references and in-text citations align.
- Personal details match user-provided background.
